- Between {words_min} and {words_max} words. This is spoken narration, so it must sound right read aloud, not read silently.
- Plain prose only. No headings, no bullet points, no stage directions, no "[pause]", no speaker labels. Every word in your reply will be spoken aloud by a synthetic voice, so anything that isn't narration ends up being read out.
- Write numbers the way they should be *spoken*: "four point two percent", not "4.2%"; "nineteen eighty", not "1980"; "two hundred fifty-two million years ago", not "252 Ma"; "magnitude nine point one", not "M9.1". Spell out units ("kilometres", not "km"). The narration model reads exactly what you write.
- Blank line between paragraphs. Each paragraph is one idea, roughly 40–90 words. These blank lines are used later to split the audio, so they matter mechanically as well as stylistically.
- Use the recurring transition "Here's how we know:" exactly once, at the moment the core evidence is revealed.
- Name at least one source out loud, in the narration itself.
- Open on the hook angle from the outline. Do not warm up, do not introduce yourself, do not say what the video will cover.
- Build a story arc. Each section raises the stakes or widens the scale on the one before. Let the event land as a felt thing first; the evidence comes right after.
- Show how we know, at least once per major section: the rock layer, the core sample, the instrument record, the study.
- Make the scale felt at least twice: translate a huge age, distance or energy into something a person can picture.
- End every major section on an open question the next section answers.
